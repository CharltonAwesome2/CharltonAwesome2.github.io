# Reflection: Agile Logbook

## 1. The Importance of Recording Development Activities

Preparing this logbook highlighted the importance of maintaining a consistent record of development activities.

During the year, I focused primarily on implementing requirements and progressing the projects assigned to me. I did not consistently document my daily activities, working hours or development decisions.

Although Git provided a useful record of changes, it did not capture everything I worked on. Research, planning, debugging and other development activities were not necessarily represented by individual commits.

Reconstructing the logbook therefore required me to combine available development records with my recollection of the work performed.

## 2. Managing Multiple Projects

Working across multiple projects required me to manage competing priorities and adjust my workload as requirements changed.

My general approach was to follow a First In, Last Out (FILO) pattern, while also considering the importance and size of new work.

Depending on the circumstances, I would complete the current task, finish an important milestone or switch to another project.

This approach allowed me to respond to changing priorities, but maintaining a clearer record of these decisions would have improved visibility into my workload and progress.

## 3. Balancing Professional and Academic Responsibilities

Throughout the reporting period, I balanced professional development responsibilities with university studies and assignments.

My available working hours varied between semesters, and university commitments occasionally affected the time I could dedicate to professional work.

This experience highlighted the importance of managing time and priorities carefully. It also reinforced the need to maintain realistic records of working hours rather than relying on estimates after the work has been completed.

## 4. Challenges and Problem-solving

One of the recurring challenges was understanding requirements and determining how systems should behave before implementing functionality.

Working independently required me to research unfamiliar areas, investigate existing implementations and identify appropriate technical solutions.

As I gained more knowledge, I sometimes recognised shortcomings in earlier implementations. Revisiting these areas provided opportunities to improve code quality, modularity and maintainability.

I learned that completing a task is not necessarily the same as implementing it well. Understanding the problem and researching established solutions are important parts of software development.

## 5. What I Would Improve

If I were to approach the reporting period again, I would maintain a more consistent development logbook.

I would record:
- The main activities completed during each working period.
- The projects and requirements receiving attention.
- Reasons for changes in priorities.
- Technical challenges and how they were addressed.
- Approximate working hours.
- Important decisions and lessons learned.

I would also improve my documentation habits so that research, implementation decisions and outstanding work could be referenced more easily.

## 6. Overall Reflection

Preparing this logbook has shown me that recording development activity is valuable beyond simply documenting the number of hours worked.

A consistent record provides a clearer view of progress, helps identify recurring challenges and makes it easier to understand how decisions were made throughout development.

Although I was able to reconstruct much of the year's activity using development history and recollection, maintaining records as the work progressed would have made this process more accurate and efficient.

Going forward, I intend to document my development activities more consistently and use those records to review progress, manage priorities and identify opportunities for improvement.
