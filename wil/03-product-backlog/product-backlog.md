# Product Backlog

## 1. Purpose

This document consolidates the requirements and development work identified across Projects A-D into retrospective product backlogs.

A product backlog is an organised list of functionality, improvements and outstanding work required for a product. It helps identify what needs to be developed, what has already been completed and what should receive attention.

The backlogs in this document were reconstructed from the development work performed, communicated requirements, available development records and personal recollection. They were not maintained as formal product backlogs during the original development process.

## 2. Priority definitions

The following priority levels are used:

- **High:** Core functionality or work essential to the primary purpose of the system.
- **Medium:** Supporting functionality that provides important additional capabilities.
- **Low:** Additional or future functionality that is less central to the implemented scope.

These priorities are retrospective classifications based on the purpose and dependencies of the functionality. They do not represent a formally recorded priority order from the original development process.

## 7. Managing priorities and outstanding work

During development, work was prioritised according to incoming requirements, the importance and size of new work, and the progress of existing tasks.

The general approach was to consider whether to:
- Complete the current task or project.
- Finish an important milestone before switching.
- Move to another project when its priority justified doing so.

This approach was generally similar to a First In, Last Out (FILO) working pattern, although priority and project size also influenced decisions.

The reconstructed backlogs provide a clearer overview of how the work can be organised. They also identify outstanding functionality that could be considered if development resumed.

## 8. Retrospective note

These backlogs were created after the development activities had taken place. The priority classifications are intended to organise the work for portfolio purposes and should not be interpreted as original planning decisions or evidence of formal backlog refinement sessions.
