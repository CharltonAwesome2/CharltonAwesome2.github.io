# Reflection: Product Backlog

## 1. Understanding the Product Backlog

Preparing the product backlogs helped me understand how requirements and development work can be organised into a prioritised list.

During development, I generally focused on implementing the requirements communicated to me and managing the work according to the priorities at the time. I did not maintain a formal product backlog.

Reconstructing the backlogs made it easier to see how the different features and requirements relate to the overall purpose of each system.

## 2. Prioritising Development Work

My approach to prioritisation was generally based on the order in which work arrived, but I also considered the importance and size of new requirements.

I generally followed a First In, Last Out (FILO) approach. However, I did not always finish one entire project before moving to another. Depending on the circumstances, I sometimes completed the current task or an important milestone before switching to a higher-priority project.

Preparing the product backlogs highlighted how these decisions could be supported by explicitly recording priorities. This would make it easier to understand why certain tasks should be addressed before others.

## 3. Managing Outstanding Requirements

Some projects were completed, while others were paused or discontinued with outstanding functionality.

The product backlog provides a useful way to distinguish between completed work, incomplete requirements and functionality planned for future development.

This is particularly important when development is interrupted. Without a clear record of outstanding requirements, it can become difficult to determine what remains to be done or where development should resume.

## 4. Adapting to Changing Priorities

Working across multiple projects required me to adjust my priorities as new requirements arose.

There were occasions when I had to decide whether to complete existing work, finish a milestone or move to another project. These decisions were influenced by the importance and size of the work involved.

A maintained product backlog would have made these decisions easier to track and communicate. It would also have provided a clearer overview of the work competing for attention.

## 5. What I Would Improve

If I were to approach these projects again, I would maintain a product backlog from the beginning of development.

I would record requirements as they are communicated, break them into manageable items, assign priorities and update their status as work progresses.

I would also review the backlog regularly to account for changing requirements and identify dependencies between tasks. This would improve visibility and make it easier to manage competing priorities.

## 6. Overall Reflection

Preparing this deliverable has helped me understand the value of maintaining an organised and prioritised list of development requirements.

Although I managed my workload independently, I can see how a product backlog would have improved my ability to track progress, manage outstanding work and make prioritisation decisions.

Going forward, I intend to use a more structured approach to recording requirements and tracking development progress, particularly when working across multiple projects.
