# Reflection: Sprint Backlog

## 1. Understanding the Sprint Backlog

Preparing this document helped me understand the difference between a product backlog and a sprint backlog.

The product backlog provides an overall list of requirements and functionality, while a sprint backlog focuses on a smaller selection of work intended to be completed during a particular development period.

This distinction makes it easier to understand how larger requirements can be divided into manageable development objectives.

## 2. Organising Development into Iterations

During development, I worked through requirements and functionality as they arose. I did not formally divide the work into Scrum sprints or establish fixed sprint durations.

Looking back at the development activities, I can identify groups of related work that could have been organised into iterations. For example, implementing a product catalogue, search functionality and product details could be treated as one development objective, while customer and administrative functionality could form another.

Organising work in this way provides a clearer view of what needs to be achieved within a particular development period.

## 3. Managing Work and Priorities

Working independently across multiple projects required me to decide how to allocate my available time.

My approach generally followed a First In, Last Out (FILO) pattern, although I also considered the importance and size of new requirements. Depending on the circumstances, I would complete existing work, finish an important milestone or switch to another project.

A sprint backlog could have helped make these decisions more structured by identifying the work selected for a particular period and making its progress easier to monitor.

## 4. Handling Incomplete Work

Not all development periods would necessarily result in every intended requirement being completed.

Several projects contained partially implemented or outstanding functionality when development was paused or discontinued.

This exercise demonstrated the importance of distinguishing between work that has been completed and work that still needs attention. If I had maintained sprint backlogs during development, it would have been easier to identify unfinished tasks and carry them forward into subsequent development periods.

## 5. What I Would Improve

If I were to approach these projects again using an iterative development process, I would establish clearer development objectives and select a manageable amount of work for each iteration.

I would also record the tasks associated with each objective, monitor their progress and review outstanding work at the end of each period.

This would provide better visibility into development progress and make it easier to manage competing priorities.

## 6. Overall Reflection

Preparing this deliverable has helped me understand how a sprint backlog can be used to organise a smaller, focused selection of development work.

Although I did not formally follow Scrum or conduct structured sprints, reconstructing the work as iterations has demonstrated how my development activities could have been organised more systematically.

Going forward, I intend to use clearer development objectives, smaller task groupings and regular progress reviews to improve how I manage software development work.
